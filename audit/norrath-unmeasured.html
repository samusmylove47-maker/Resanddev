01

The verdict

EQL Source is a journal competing in a market that rewards infrastructure. It has the best prose and the worst coverage of any site in its field. That is not a stable position, and the window in which it can be corrected is measured in weeks.

The craft here is real and I want to be precise about it before I take it apart. The palette is derived from DXT1 endpoint colours sampled out of the game's own .s3d archives — 2.6 million samples — because somebody proved the site was the wrong temperature rather than guessing. Every text token carries its measured contrast ratio in a comment. The changelog publishes corrections that make the project look worse, by name. The Sky Ledger refuses to print a drop rate it cannot measure and prints a bound instead. I have audited a great many sites. Almost none of them are built by someone who thinks this hard.

And almost none of that reaches a new visitor, because the first screen of eqlsource.com is a manifesto about epistemology rendered in vector line art, and the person who arrived wants to know where a Dark Reaver drops.

Here is the uncomfortable arithmetic. EverQuest Legends launched on 28 July 2026. In the five weeks since, at least seven independent fan sites have shipped for it. One of them, EQLBase, lists 9,360 items, 1,448 spells and 568 zones and calls itself "VERY early alpha." Your Index lists 435 items across 13 zones. Another, EQ Legends Tools, ships a Plane of Sky tracker citing the identical 95 rewards and 128 turn-in items your Sky Ledger is built on — the same feature, shipped against the same numbers, with an inventory-file upload that is easier to use than reading a combat log. A third ships a 3D zone atlas, while your own changelog records that you deleted your 3D model.

You are not losing on quality. You are losing on surface area and on presence, and in a land grab those are the only two things that compound.

The single most important sentence in this document

Your sourcing standard is not a reason to publish less. It is the licence to publish far more than anyone else can.

Every rival importing 9,000 mined items is quietly lying about their confidence in them. You built a five-tier badge system that would let you import the same 9,000 and mark each one honestly — Tier 4, mined snapshot, stale after a patch — while your 435 surveyed entries stand at Tier 1 and 2 and visibly outrank them. You have been treating the standard as a brake. It is the only engine on the site that can outrun the field.

02

What you actually built

Read blind, from the outside, with no access to your repository: a hand-authored static site of 715 pages — 441 item pages, 232 named-mob pages, 13 dungeon surveys, 8 tools, 7 explainers and a single raid zone. No framework, no database, no build-visible JavaScript beyond a theme toggle and a small site script. Self-hosted Cinzel and IBM Plex Mono. A 1,445-line stylesheet carrying 107 custom properties and an unusual amount of documented reasoning.

Navigation reads Dungeons · Raids · Tools · The Index · Learn · Accuracy · Search. The tools are the Index, Sky Ledger, 50 Upgrades, a gap engine, a lockout tracker, EQLS Auras, a race-unlock tracker, a race and primary calculator, and a faction impact checker. State is packed into the URL rather than a server. Logs are parsed in the browser and never uploaded. There are no accounts and no analytics of any kind.

What is genuinely excellent, and worth defending

▸ KEEP

The prose

"An empty log is not evidence that something is available." Nobody in this genre writes like this. It is the most differentiated asset you own and it cannot be cloned by a rival in a weekend.

▸ KEEP

The tier system

Five sourcing tiers with badges that travel to every page a claim appears on. No competitor has an answer to "how confident are you." Scale it, do not soften it.

▸ KEEP

Measured colour

A palette sampled from the game's own textures, and hairlines raised to clear 2.25:1 because an audit proved the old ones were invisible. This is real engineering.

▸ KEEP

Bounds, not zeros

Printing <28% · 0/9 instead of 0% is the most intellectually honest thing on any MMO database anywhere. It is also, as it happens, your path to a moat. See §06.

03

Findings

Ordered by severity. Every number below was measured against the live site on 31 August 2026, not inferred.

F01

You are invisible in a genre that runs on iconsCritical

There is not one <img> element on your homepage. Not an item icon, not a spell icon, not a mob portrait, not a screenshot, not a piece of character art. An MMO player's memory is visual — they recall the orange sword icon long before they recall "A Dark Reaver." Wowhead's entire interface is an icon grid. Every one of your seven rivals ships item icons.

Correction, 31 Aug 2026 — the struck sentences are wrong. I tested for <img> and reported the result as if I had tested for imagery. Your homepage carries two autoplaying <video> elements — 1.83 MB of gameplay footage of Sky Ledger and Auras — behind two JPEG posters totalling 360,099 B. Those posters are screenshots. Found on re-verification, by me, after a peer independently confirmed the null and I went looking for the way my own method could have produced it.

What survives is narrower and, I think, more damning: the imagery is all in the marketing and none of it is in the data. You can evidently produce polished video of your own tools. None of that capability reaches the surfaces players actually use.

This is the single highest-leverage fix available to you, and — this is the maddening part — you already own the hard half of it. Your _build/palette.py already opens the game's .s3d archives and decodes DXT1 texture blocks. Icons live in those same archives. The pipeline that proved your palette was the wrong temperature is the pipeline that can emit four thousand WebP icons in one pass.

Re-measured 31 Aug: homepage — 0 <img>, 0 <picture>, 0 CSS url(), but 2 <video> + 2 JPEG posters. · tools/index-search.html (the Index, 225,571 B) — 0 images of any kind across 435 rows. · Item pages — exactly 1 image each, the floor-plan locator SVG, which is a genuinely good idea nobody else has. 0 item icons anywhere on the site.

F02

Your coverage is a rounding errorCritical

435 indexed items against EQLBase's 9,360 is 4.7%. Thirteen zones against 568 is 2.3%. A reference work that answers one question in twenty is not a reference work; it is a sample. The player who misses twice stops coming, and they do not come back to check whether you improved.

Depth without breadth loses to breadth without depth, every time, in this specific category. That is not a statement about what should win.

Measured: sitemap.xml → 715 URLs. 441 /items/, 232 /named/, 13 /dungeons/, 8 /tools/, 7 /learn/, 1 /raids/.

F03

Six sevenths of your homepage is decorationWarning

The homepage is 241,709 bytes. Sixteen inline SVGs account for 206,316 of them — 85.4%. The hero alone is 21,607 bytes of hand-authored path data across 96 paths. The actual text content of the page, the part a human reads, is roughly four kilobytes.

You are spending fifty bytes of decoration for every byte of value… on a phone on hotel wifi that is a blank screen for several seconds.

Correction, 31 Aug 2026 — the composition figure holds, the consequence does not. I asserted a load-time impact and never measured transfer. The page is served over HTTP/2 with Brotli: 241,709 B becomes 39,547 B on the wire, a 6.1:1 ratio, because repeated SVG path data is exactly what compresses best. 39.5 KB is not a blank screen. Severity drops from Critical to Warning, and the reason changes: this is an authoring, maintenance and DOM-size cost, not a bandwidth one.

And the real byte story is one I missed entirely. The homepage pulls 2.19 MB of media — two autoplaying MP4 trailers (971,771 B and 859,203 B) behind two JPEG posters (180,943 B and 179,156 B). That is 55× the compressed HTML. The heavy thing on this page was never the SVG.

Measured: 241,709 B uncompressed · 206,316 B SVG · 16 elements · largest 21,607 B / 96 paths · 39,547 B transferred (br) · plus 2,191,073 B of media. Composition independently confirmed from the repository by a second party using a different instrument; all four figures matched exactly.

F04

You are not where your community livesCritical

There is no Discord. There is no forum, no comment thread, no account, no way to contribute that does not require a GitHub account and the willingness to file a YAML issue template. That last condition excludes, conservatively, ninety-five percent of the people who play this game.

You know this. Your own changelog says, verbatim: "EverQuest communities coordinate in Discord, and a link with no card is a link nobody opens." You diagnosed the problem exactly and then fixed only the Open Graph tags. EQLBase has a Discord. EQ Legends Tools has a Discord and credits submissions through it. You are the only serious project in this field with no room of its own.

Measured: across every page fetched — one GitHub issue link, one GitHub release link, zero Discord/Reddit/YouTube/Twitch URLs.

F05

You market your methodology instead of your utilitySerious

The hero says "Norrath, measured," and then three paragraphs about sourcing discipline, then four counters, then thirteen zone cards with ZEM values, and only then the tools. A first-time visitor gets an epistemology lecture before a search box.

Nobody arrives at a fan site wanting to know how you know. They arrive wanting to know where the thing drops. They come to trust your rigour on the second visit, having been helped on the first. You have inverted the order, and the section header "Start here" appears roughly two thousand pixels below the top of the page.

F06

Your best tool is not on your site, and your homepage contradicts itselfSerious

The hero counter reads "435 items indexed." Five hundred pixels below, the 50 Upgrades panel reads "It holds 3,663 items, 1,713 of them carrying stat values." Both statements are on the same screen. A visitor who notices concludes you cannot count; a visitor who does not notice simply absorbs the smaller number.

The reason is architectural: 50 Upgrades is "built and hosted in its own repository," as is Sky Ledger. Your most impressive work lives off your domain, earning no traffic, no links and no search authority for eqlsource.com. You have the data for 3,663 items and an Index that exposes 435 of them.

The gap is not a data problem. It is a plumbing problem, and it is the cheapest large win on this list.

F07

Nothing brings anyone back tomorrowSerious

No news, no patch tracking, no economy, no profiles, no leaderboards, no subscriptions, no notifications. The site is a book. Books get read once.

Every site that has won this category has a daily hook: Wowhead has news, Raider.IO has your score, poe.ninja has today's prices, Warcraft Logs has last night's parse. You have URL-packed trackers that live in the visitor's bookmarks rather than on your domain — an admirable privacy stance that also means a returning user may never load your site again.

F08

Your tools are named for insidersSerious

Sky Ledger · Gap engine · 50 Upgrades · The Index · Accuracy · Motes · Contamination. Not one of these tells a stranger what it does. Compare the field: "Item Planner," "Build Planner," "Spell Search," "BiS Weapons Search," "Plane of Sky Quest Tracker."

Poetic naming is a luxury of the incumbent. Wowhead can call something whatever it likes because everyone already knows. You are not the incumbent yet. Names should be descriptions now and can become poetry later.

F09

You are flying blindSerious

No analytics of any kind — no Plausible, no Umami, not even self-hosted log aggregation surfaced anywhere. You have 715 pages and no idea which ones anyone reads, which searches return nothing, or where visitors give up.

This is a defensible ethical position taken too far. Privacy-respecting, cookieless, self-hosted analytics measure pages, not people, and violate nothing you have promised. Without them, every decision below is a guess — including, I would gently note, your own decisions about what to build next.

F10

A 100.5 MB download, in 2026Warning

The Sky Ledger overlay ships as a hundred-megabyte Windows zip from a GitHub release. That is a runtime, not a tool. The in-browser version is 178 KB and is the one you should lead with in every context; the overlay should be the reward for a user already convinced, not the headline call to action.

F11

You deleted the 3D while a rival shipped itWarning

Your changelog, 20 August: "Our share cards advertised a 3D model we deleted, and walls we never had." … you threw the asset away for accuracy reasons that a "geometry: Tier 4, mined" badge would have resolved in a line of markup.

Correction, 31 Aug 2026 — the mechanism claim is withdrawn. I read a one-line headline in the homepage's "What changed" strip and treated it as the reason. It is not. Following sources.html#changelog — a link I had and did not open — the 20 Aug entry is a correction to share cards, and it covers two unrelated errors: a raids card still advertising a model already withdrawn on 17 August, and a dungeons card claiming "walls drawn from the game's own files" when the geometry holds walkable-floor outlines and "the word 'wall' does not appear anywhere in the geometry we hold." I merged two corrections into one sentence and inferred a motive from the merger. The public record does not state why the model was withdrawn at all.

Director-supplied, 31 Aug, not independently verified — the model was withdrawn because the tactic it depicted (pulling the boss down to island 7) was inherited Project 1999 text, contradicted by a collaborator who has killed that boss about ten times and always where it spawns. The stated principle: "a drawing is an assertion" — a model carries more conviction than the same claim in prose, so it needs more evidence, not less. That is coherent with this site's own published tiers, where T5 is "Inherited classic prose · Project 1999 text." A geometry badge would not have fixed a false tactic, because the falsehood was in the fight, not the walls. I accept the correction.

What stands, as a separate argument: you derive walkable-floor outlines from the game's own meshes, and EQL Tools now leads with "Explore Norrath in 3D." The pipeline exists and the field has moved. Ship a zone atlas — but the principle above is the design constraint, not an objection: let it assert geometry only. Walkable-floor outlines, tier-badged, with recorded /loc pins. No routes, no pull paths, no tactics — those are prose claims and must not borrow a drawing's authority.

F12

Per-page inline stylesheetsWarning

Item pages carry their own <style> block — roughly 4 KB of rules duplicated across 441 pages that could be cached once. Minor beside the rest of this list, but it is the same instinct as the hero SVG: paying repeatedly for something that should be paid for once.

F13

Your Cache-Control headers are concatenated, not replacedSerious

Added 31 Aug 2026, after delivery. Nobody asked for this one.

Every static asset returns a single Cache-Control line carrying two or three stacked, conflicting policies:

assets/site.css, site.js, fonts/fonts.css →
public, max-age=0, must-revalidate, public, max-age=3600

assets/media/*.jpg →
public, max-age=0, must-revalidate, public, max-age=3600, public, max-age=31536000, immutable

The restrictive policy is first and wins. Your filenames (site.css?v=0ebb828c, sky-ledger-poster.af5c97c2.jpg) are content-hashed, so a year-long immutable cache was plainly intended — and the header defeats it. The immutable directive you wrote is never reached.

Stated precisely, because I have already overstated one performance claim in this document: ETags are present and revalidation returns 304 at 0 bytes, so the cost is latency, not bandwidth — one conditional round-trip per asset per navigation, on a site where nothing about those assets has changed. Cheap to fix, and it makes the caching you already designed actually work.

The four laws these sites obey and you do not

LAW 01

The icon is the interface

Players navigate by image before text. A database without icons reads as a spreadsheet, and a spreadsheet is something you close.

LAW 02

The tooltip is the distribution

The winners stopped being websites and became a layer other people's pages render. Wowhead's embed is why it is unkillable.

LAW 03

Identity creates return

A profile, a score, a saved set — something the player owns and points at. Bookmarked URL state is not ownership; it is a receipt.

LAW 04

The community must be able to write

Read-only sites lose to editable ones on a long enough timeline. 2.6 million edits is not a team; it is a permission model.

05

The proposal

Five pillars. Ordered so that each one makes the next cheaper. Items marked KEYSTONE are the ones that change the trajectory; everything else is support.

Pillar I

The visual layer — stop being invisible

The goal is that a stranger landing on any page of this site in 2026 recognises within one second that it is about a game, and within three that it is the best-made site about that game. Right now it reads, at a glance, as a technical standards document.

  • Keystone

    Extract every icon from the client archives

    Extend the .s3d DXT1 decoder you already wrote for the palette study to emit item, spell and status icons as WebP, plus a sprite atlas. Put an icon on every row of the Index, every drop list, every planner slot, every result. This is one build step against tooling you already own, and it is worth more than the next ten things on this list combined.

  • Keystone

    Ship an embeddable tooltip

    A sub-5 KB script that turns any eqlsource.com/items/… link on any forum, wiki or Discord-adjacent page into a rich card with icon, stats, drop source and the sourcing tier badge. This is how Wowhead became infrastructure. It also carries your one real differentiator — confidence — to every place your link is pasted.

  • Rebuild the hero around the product

    Delete the 206 KB of path data. Lead with a search field that actually searches, over a cheap canvas or WebGL ground. The current hero costs fifty times what it communicates.

  • Restore 3D, badged honestly

    A WebGL zone atlas: orbit, floor toggles, named-mob pins plotted from recorded /loc, drop overlays on click. You already derive the floor plans from mesh. Mark the geometry Tier 4 and ship it — that is precisely what the tier system is for.

  • Rarity, tier and slot colour on every item

    Encode two dimensions at once: item quality and source confidence. Nobody else in the genre can show the second one.

  • Faces for the 232 named

    Rendered mob portraits from the client models, and a zone-entrance screenshot on every survey. Thirteen dungeons currently open with no photograph of the place.

Pillar II

The data layer — stop being a rounding error

Coverage is the price of admission and you have not paid it. The tier system is what lets you pay it without compromising anything you have promised.

  • Keystone

    Bulk-import to parity, badged at Tier 4

    Ingest the full client item, spell, NPC, zone and faction tables. Every imported row enters at Tier 4 — "mined snapshot, stale after a patch" — dated, and visibly outranked by your surveyed entries. You go from 4.7% coverage to 100% overnight and become the only site in the field that tells you which rows to trust. This is not a compromise of the standard. It is the standard finally doing work.

  • Bring 50 Upgrades home

    Move the planner onto the domain and wire it to the same item records as the Index. One canonical item table ends the 435-versus-3,663 contradiction, consolidates your search authority, and makes every planner slot a link into a real item page.

  • Extend past items

    Spells, alternate advancement, factions, quests, tradeskills, vendors, bestiary. Every rival already spans these; you cover one axis of seven.

  • Make surveying visibly progressive

    Show 13 of 568 zones surveyed as a filling progress bar rather than as a silent absence. An honest gap, displayed, is an invitation to help. An undisplayed gap is just a missing page.

Pillar III

The return hook — and the one idea that wins outright

This is the section to read twice. Everything above gets you to parity with EQLBase. This is what puts you somewhere none of them can follow.

The moat

Be the only game database in the world whose drop rates carry error bars.

Drop rates on every MMO database ever built are folklore. Allakhazam's are twenty-year-old guesses; Wowhead's are addon-scraped with no interval published; every EQL rival simply omits them. Meanwhile you already print <28% · 0/9 instead of 0% because you refuse to state what you have not measured.

Turn that scruple into the product. Opt-in pooled log donation, parsed client-side, submitting only anonymous kill-and-drop counts — never chat, never character names, never a raw log. Publish every rate as a point estimate with a Wilson confidence interval, a sample size, a date, and a tier badge that rises from Tier 4 to Tier 1 as n grows. A rate that reads 14.2% ± 3.1 · n=1,847 · Tier 1 is a claim no other site in this genre is structurally capable of making, because none of them have spent five weeks building the epistemics to support it.

It is a daily-return hook, a contribution ramp, a share object and a defensible technical moat, and it is the natural conclusion of work you have already done.

  • Character profiles, opt-in and shareable

    A URL a player owns and pastes in guild chat. Keep the no-account default; offer a claim-this-profile upgrade for those who want persistence. Identity is Law 03 and you currently satisfy none of it.

  • Patch diffs — "what the game changed"

    You already run a public changelog for the site. Run one for Norrath: auto-diff client data between builds and publish what moved — stats, spawns, loot tables, zone values. It is the single most shareable artefact a fan site can produce on patch day, it is fully automatable, and no EQL site is doing it.

  • Server progression board

    First kills, unlock pace, class-combination distribution. Aggregate, anonymous, and a reason to check in on a Tuesday.

Pillar IV

The community layer — be reachable

  • This week

    Open a Discord and put a bot in it

    A bot that answers /item Dark Reaver inline with an icon, stats, drop source and tier badge puts your database inside every guild server in the game. You correctly identified that this community coordinates in Discord. Go there.

  • Web-based finding submission

    Five fields, no GitHub account, screenshot upload, routed into the same triage you already run. Keep the log-privacy warning verbatim — it is excellent.

  • Community annotations, tier-governed

    Comments are how Wowhead survived and how Allakhazam rotted. You have the only credible answer anyone has proposed: submissions enter at Tier 5, are promoted to Tier 3 on corroboration and Tier 1 on verification, and every annotation displays its tier. You can take user-generated content without becoming a rumour mill, because you built the grading system first.

  • A contributor wall

    You already credit finders by name — that instinct is right. Make it a page, a count and a badge. People contribute to things that visibly remember them.

Pillar V

The editorial reframe — sell the tool, keep the voice

  • Make the front door a search box

    Search first, tools second, atlas third, methodology in a badge that links to the full standard. The sourcing story is your closing argument, not your opening one.

  • Rename every tool to say what it does

    Sky Ledger → Plane of Sky Tracker · 50 Upgrades → Gear Planner · The Index → Item & Mob Database · Gap engine → Upgrade Finder · Accuracy → How We Source. Keep the poetic names as subtitles. Earn them back once you are the default.

  • Ship a command palette

    ⌘K from any page, searching items, mobs, zones and tools. Cheap, and it makes a static site feel like an application.

  • Keep the long-form exactly as it is

    "Is it still true?", "Contamination", "Reading a floor plan" — this writing is the best thing on the site and the hardest thing to copy. It belongs in Learn, reached by people you have already helped. Do not dilute it, and do not lead with it.

08

Closing

The hardest thing about this audit is that almost every criticism in it is a criticism of restraint — and restraint is the reason the site is good.

You did not ship icons because you had not verified them. You did not import nine thousand items because you could not source them. You deleted the 3D model because the walls were wrong. You will not take comments because comments rot. Every one of those was a defensible call made for an honourable reason, and collectively they have produced a site that is more careful than any competitor and less useful than all of them.

The way out is not to abandon the standard. It is to notice that the standard was built precisely so that you could publish uncertain things honestly — and then to actually publish them. Tier 4 is not a confession. It is a licence. Use it, and you can have both the coverage of EQLBase and a credibility none of them can construct, because they did not do the hard part first. You did.

EverQuest Legends is thirty-four days old. In ninety days one of these eight sites will be the one people link in Discord without thinking, and the rest will be alternatives. You are currently in second place on craft and last place on everything that decides it. That is a fixable position, and it will not be fixable for long.

Correction

F02 compared unlike units, and it was the loudest thing in this document.

I set 435 verified items against EQLBase's 9,360 and called the result 4.7% coverage. That is not a like-for-like comparison. 9,360 is a count of records — largely classic-EverQuest baseline data imported wholesale — of which an unpublished fraction has been confirmed to exist in Legends. 435 is a count of verified items. Dividing one by the other produces a number that means nothing, and I led with it.

The evidence, in competitors' own words

This is not a charitable reading offered on request. It is what the field says about itself.

E01

Gnoll Guard, 12 August 2026Self-reported

A competing EQL item database published this about its own pages: "our item pages have been serving stats scraped from a classic-EverQuest reference wiki instead of the data we captured from Legends itself."

Their own figures: 502 items that exist in Legends were missing entirely · 782 items showed no stats when the real numbers were on hand · 186 items showed stats that disagreed with what the game actually reports. A follow-up on 16 Aug found 27 more with misread penalty stats. Class and race restrictions, they note, still come from the scrape.

Their fix — read captured data first, fall back to the wiki, and "where the two disagree, the game wins" — is the correct fix, published honestly. It is also a precise description of the failure mode you deleted eleven thousand rows to avoid.

E02

EQLBase publishes the total and withholds the splitStructural

Its own words: "Baseline records remain labeled until Legends evidence confirms them." So the site knows the distinction and encodes it internally with an ✓ EQL Verified filter.

But the homepage advertises 9,360 Items · 1,448 Spells · 568 Zones with no breakdown whatsoever of how many are confirmed. The number a visitor is recruited by is the number that has not been checked. It also self-describes as "VERY early alpha."

What the correction does not rescue

Being right about the unit does not make the position safe, and I would be doing you no favours by trading one bad number for a comfortable one.

  • The true denominator is still unknown — to me, and possibly to you

    Nobody in this audit established how many items actually exist in EverQuest Legends. Gnoll Guard's disclosure implies a real corpus in the low thousands. Until that denominator is published, "435" and "9,360" are both unanchored, and the side with the bigger unanchored number wins by default.

  • Your own planner holds 3,663 items

    This is the question I cannot answer from outside, and it decides everything below. If those 3,663 survived the same verification the 435 did, then your headline number is 3,663, your Index is publishing 12% of your own verified corpus, and the fix is plumbing rather than research. If they did not, then the planner is doing internally what this section criticises externally. One of those is a very good day's work. The other is F06 with worse implications.

  • An empty result is still an empty result

    The player who searches for a real item and finds nothing does not learn that you were being rigorous. They learn that the site does not have it. Correctness explains the gap; it does not close it, and it does not retain the user.

The strategy: stop competing on a unit you must lose

You are reporting a numerator with no denominator, in a category where the only visible metric rewards the thing you deliberately refused to do. That is not a marketing problem to be solved with better phrasing. It is the wrong unit. Change the unit and you hold the largest number on the field.

  • Move 01

    Lead with the deletions

    Your instinct is right and it is the strongest number you own. "11,000+ classic-EverQuest entries removed as not present in Legends" is a bigger number than 9,360. It is earned, it is verifiable from your own changelog, and no rival can match it without conceding why they should have. Publish the exact figure, the date, and the method.

  • Move 02

    Report a fraction, never a count

    "435 of 447 items in the surveyed zones — 97%" is unassailable in a way that "435 items" never will be. A count invites the comparison you lose; a percentage with a stated denominator invites the comparison nobody else can even attempt, because none of them can name their denominator.

  • Move 03

    Publish the falsification rate — The Concordance

    Sample n entries at random from each EQL database, check each against the game, and publish the disagreement rate with full methodology, dates and per-entry evidence. Re-run monthly. This is your sourcing standard pointed outward, and it is the single move that converts their headline number from an asset into a liability — permanently, and without you ever having to argue about it again.

    Three rules make it an institution rather than a hit piece, and without all three it will backfire on you specifically, because your credibility is the entire product: publish your own error rate in the same table; give every site advance notice and a correction window before publishing; and lead with the fact that Gnoll Guard found and disclosed its own problem, which is creditable behaviour and should be named as such. You already flagged unease about criticising another project publicly. Method, right of reply, and self-inclusion are what separate a benchmark from an attack.

  • Move 04

    Own the extraction surface

    You are right that the qualifying judgement is now often made by a machine reading a snippet. That surface is small and specific, and you control all of it: the <title>, the meta description, the first ~200 words of the page, and structured data. Your description currently sells method and carries no number at all.

    Concretely: put the deletion figure in the meta description and the opening sentence, in short declarative clauses that survive being chunked. Add llms.txt at the root. Mark the database up as schema.org Dataset with measurementTechnique and variableMeasured — and consider ClaimReview, which exists precisely to encode fact-checked claims and maps onto your five tiers almost exactly. Nobody in games uses it. Then write one canonical page — /why-our-number-is-smaller — numbers first, no preamble, built to be quoted verbatim by a model that reads three paragraphs and moves on.

  • Move 05

    Name the unit and make the field adopt it

    Say records and verified entries everywhere, relentlessly, until the community says it too. Whoever defines the vocabulary wins every future comparison before it starts. Wowhead never had to defend its count because it defined what counting meant.

The revised position

You are not behind on coverage. You are behind on legibility.

The audit's other eleven findings stand unchanged — the missing icons, the 85.4% decorative payload, the absent Discord, the unmeasured traffic, the off-domain tools, the names that explain nothing. But F02 was wrong, and it was wrong in the direction that would have pushed you to abandon the one discipline that makes this site worth defending. Had you taken my advice as written, you would have re-imported the eleven thousand rows you were right to delete.

Publish the denominator, publish the deletions, publish the error rates — yours included — and the number stops being an argument you are losing and becomes the argument you are winning.
